AUTOMATED CABLE SPECIMEN PREPARATION SYSTEM 
1. Existing Technology and Current State
Manual Specimen Preparation
The conventional cable specimen preparation process is largely manual and involves multiple operations such as cutting, straightening, insulation/sheath stripping, slicing, and specimen formation. A typical specimen may require approximately 
10 minutes
 to prepare, with significant operator-dependent variability and errors.
Industrial Wire Stripping Machines
Commercial wire-processing and stripping machines can perform high-speed cutting and insulation stripping. However, these systems are primarily designed for wire processing and generally do not provide an integrated solution for 
test-specimen preparation, dimensional verification, or standards-specific specimen formation
.
Existing Automated Prototype
A recently reported automated cable specimen preparation prototype integrates multiple preparation stages and reduces the preparation time to approximately 
142 seconds per specimen
. The reported system demonstrates good repeatability, with a 
Cpk
 value of approximately 1.71
. However, the system does not incorporate comprehensive real-time material sensing, adaptive cutting-depth control, or intelligent closed-loop quality verification.

3. Proposed Novelty
The proposed system aims to integrate multiple technologies into a 
single adaptive and standards-aware automated cable specimen preparation platform
.
The key novelty is the combination of:
Real-time cable diameter measurement and material sensing
Adaptive blade-depth control to minimize conductor damage
Automated preparation of standards-compliant dumbbell specimens
Inline dimensional and surface-quality verification before final specimen release
Industry 4.0 connectivity for data logging, traceability, and laboratory integration
RFID-based specimen identification and traceability
Integration with LIMS for automated test-data management
Rather than simply automating individual cutting or stripping operations, the proposed system focuses on 
closed-loop, sensor-driven specimen preparation with automated quality assurance and standards-based parameter selection
.
4. Recommended System Architecture
The proposed system can be organized into five major processing stages.
Stage 1 – Feed and Measure
The cable specimen is automatically fed into the system using controlled rollers.
A 
laser diameter sensor
 continuously measures the cable diameter. At the same stage, a material-identification module can 
analyze
 the cable insulation/sheath characteristics and classify the material, such as:
PVC
XLPE
HDPE
Other relevant polymeric insulation/sheath materials
The measured diameter and identified material are used by the controller to determine suitable processing parameters.
Stage 2 – Cutting and Adaptive Stripping
A servo-controlled cutting mechanism performs the required cutting and stripping operations.
The system automatically adjusts the blade position according to the measured cable diameter and material properties. 
Ultrasonic sensing and encoder feedback
 can be used to monitor the cutting process and provide closed-loop control of blade depth.
The objective is to remove the insulation or sheath while minimizing the possibility of conductor damage or unintended material removal.
Stage 3 – Stripping and Flattening
After stripping, the specimen is transferred through a controlled roller mechanism.
Reverse-roller or controlled-roller motion can be used to straighten and flatten the prepared material. A controlled weighting or pressing mechanism can provide consistent flattening conditions.
This stage aims to reduce operator-dependent variation before the specimen proceeds to slicing and final specimen formation.
Stage 4 – Slicing and Dumbbell Specimen Formation
The prepared material is positioned accurately for longitudinal slicing.
A precision longitudinal cutting mechanism separates the required specimen section, after which an automated 
dumbbell-shaped specimen forming/stamping mechanism
 produces the required specimen geometry.
The die or forming parameters can be selected automatically according to the applicable testing standard and specimen type.
Stage 5 – Inline Verification
Before the specimen is released for testing, an industrial camera-based vision system performs automated quality inspection.
The system can verify parameters such as:
Specimen length
Width
Thickness-related dimensional features
Gauge length
Dumbbell geometry
Cutting-edge quality
Visible conductor damage
Surface defects
Alignment and positioning
Specimens that satisfy the programmed acceptance criteria are released automatically, while defective specimens are identified and rejected or flagged for operator review.

6. Key Research Questions
The development of the system should investigate the following research questions:
Can sensor-based measurement achieve the required cutting-depth accuracy across different cable insulation and sheath materials such as PVC, XLPE, and HDPE?
Which sensing and machine-learning approach provides reliable real-time identification of cable insulation/sheath materials?
Can adaptive cutting-depth control significantly reduce conductor damage compared with fixed-depth or manually adjusted stripping?
Can sensor fusion between diameter measurement, material identification, encoder feedback, and cutting-force/depth information improve process repeatability?
Can an inline machine-vision system reliably detect dimensional deviations and visible defects before the specimen enters the testing stage?
Can automated standards-based parameter selection reduce operator-dependent variation in specimen preparation?
Can the complete automated workflow reduce specimen preparation time while maintaining or improving dimensional accuracy and repeatability?

Overall R&D Opportunity
The individual technologies required for automated cable specimen preparation already exist in different forms, including 
automated wire processing, laser measurement, machine vision, adaptive cutting, servo control, material sensing, and industrial data connectivity
.
However, the major R&D opportunity lies in integrating these technologies into a 
single closed-loop, adaptive, standards-aware cable specimen preparation system
.
The proposed system would move the process from conventional 
manual and operator-dependent specimen preparation
 toward an 
intelligent automated workflow
 consisting of:
Measure → Identify → Adapt → Cut → Strip → Flatten → Slice → Form → Verify → Trace
The core research contribution is therefore not simply the automation of individual operations, but the development of a 
sensor-fusion and closed-loop control architecture capable of adapting the preparation process to cable geometry and material while automatically verifying the resulting specimen against predefined standards-based requirements
.
